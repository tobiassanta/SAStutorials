Before you start

  • Download and install SasView.

  • It is required that you do the Spheres tutorial and the Primary Data Analysis tutorial. We recommend that you also do the Structure Factors tutorial and the Polydispersity tutorial.

Learning outcomes

After completing this tutorial, you will be able to perform analysis of casein micelle data, including
  • Upload and use a custom Sasview model for SAXS data analysis.
  • Estimate physically reasonable starting values before fitting
  • Identify which parameters should be fitted and which should remain fixed.
  • Assess whether a fit is statistically and physically meaningful.
  • Use residuals, $\chi^2$ and parameter correlations to evaluate the reliability of a SAXS model fit.

Introductory remarks

Casein micelles are colloidal protein structures consisting primarily of four different casein proteins. These micelles are stabilized by calcium phosphate clusters, forming a complex architecture. The structure is highly dynamic and sensitive to environmental factors. Their SAXS profiles therefore cannot be interpreted by simply fitting a sphere model. Instead, successful modelling requires combining physical knowledge of the system with an iterative fitting strategy.

Part I: The casein micelle model in SasView

The casein micelle model which we will be using in this tutorial, will be a model in which the total scattering, $I_{total}$, is given as

    $$I_{total}=I_{aggr}+I_{micelle}+I_{int}+I_{P}+I_{C}+I_{PC}+I_{L}+I_{B},$$
where the first two terms are contributions from casein micelle aggregates and the overall casein micelle, respectively (click here for a detailed description). To fit the data to our model, follow the steps in the box below.
    How to get ready for the casein micelle model fitting:
  1. Open SasView and click the "Fitting" panel in the top left corner.
  2. Choose the "Manage Custom Models" panel and click the "Add File..." button.
  3. Find the casein micelle model on your PC and choose it.
  4. Press "Close".
  5. Load your data using the "Load Data" button in the Data Explorer panel and click the button that says "Send to" (fitting).

Part II: Preparing the fit

Before jumping straight into fitting of the SAXS curve, start by inspecting the data. The purpose of inspecting the curve is not to determine every model parameter directly from the data, but instead to identify the main structural features and regions of the data such as aggregation at low $q$, broad peaks, noisy regions etc. Furthermore, not every parameter in the model should be estimated directly from the SAXS data. Several parameters describe known sample properties rather than structural features. These should normally be obtained from the sample preparation or from literature values and kept fixed during fitting. Some examples from the model that are known beforehand and hence should not be fitted are listed below.

Parameter Description Typical source Fit?
c_P Protein concentration Measured experimentally No
c_C Calcium phosphate concentration Sample composition No
MW_P Protein molecular weight Literature No
rho_C Calcium phosphate density Literature No

The remaining parameters describe the structural features of the casein micelle. Unlike the parameters above, they cannot be obtained directly from the sample preparation and therefore requires reasonable estimates before fitting. Some examples are listed below.

Parameter Description
scale_power Intensity of the aggregate contribution
power Slope of the aggregate contribution
R_L Average radius of the casein micelles
sigma_L Polydispersity of the casein micelles
R_int Average radius of the intermediate structures
sigma_int Polydispersity of the intermediate structures
background Constant background intensity

Although the model contains many parameters, only a subset should initially be fitted (i.e. do not fit all 30 parameters initially). The recommended strategy is to begin with parameters describing the largest structural features and gradually include additional parameters as required. This minimizes parameter correlation and improves convergence.

Part III: Stepwise fitting of casein micelle data

We will now be jumping into the actual procedure of fitting casein micelle SAXS data. To do this, choose the "Plugin Models" category and the "casein_micelle" model name in the fit window of SasView. A number of fitting parameters will be listed in the window and we will now proceed with a step-by-step fitting strategy of the data. Before fitting a parameter, change its value manually and press "Compute". Observe which part of the scattering curve changes. This provides an indication of the q-range to which the parameter is sensitive and helps identify a physically reasonable starting value. It is important to not use the built-in SasView scale parameter for this model.

    Stepwise fitting strategy of casein micelle SAXS data:
  1. Manually adjust background to describe the high-q baseline.
  2. Adjust scale_power and, if necessary, power to describe the low-q upturn.
  3. Fit these nuisance/background contributions.
  4. Manually vary the average micelle radius, R_L and observe which region changes.
  5. Fit R_L.
  6. Add sigma_L.
  7. Proceed to R_int then sigma_int.
  8. Inspect residuals before introducing additional parameters.
